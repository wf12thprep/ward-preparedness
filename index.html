After an earthquake: the first hour

Southern Utah sits on active faults, including the Hurricane Fault. A strong earthquake is the emergency most likely to affect all of us at once. Phones, power, and roads may be down. Here is what we will each do.

  1. Drop, cover, and hold on until the shaking stops. Expect aftershocks.
  2. Check your household for injuries. Give first aid where you can.
  3. Check for hazards. Turn off the gas only if you smell gas or hear hissing. Watch for downed power lines and broken glass.
  4. Put your sign in the front window so neighbors and your ward contact can see it from the street:
    OKHELP
  5. Check on the neighbors around you, especially anyone who lives alone or has limited mobility.
  6. Report to your ward contact or come to the gathering point (below). Text instead of calling; texts get through when calls don't.
  7. Listen for official updates on a battery or crank radio: KDXU 890 AM, the area's main emergency alert station, or NOAA Weather Radio on 162.475 MHz.

Call 911 only for life-threatening emergencies so lines stay open for the people who need them most.

Where to gather

If you can't reach anyone, or you need help, come to the gathering point. Leaders will check here first. After an earthquake, wait outside in the open, away from buildings and power lines.

Get official alerts on your phone

Washington County Emergency Management sends text alerts. Text a keyword to 888777:

  • WASHCOES for general emergency updates
  • WASHCOFIRE for fire updates
  • WCFLOOD for flood warnings

For evacuations, the county uses the free Genasys Protect app: look up your evacuation zone before you need it. More at Washington County Emergency Management.

Other emergencies

Wildfire or evacuation

Keep a go-bag by the door with medications, documents, chargers, and a change of clothes. Leave as soon as you're told to; don't wait. Let your ward contact know where you went.

Extreme heat or summer power outage

A summer outage can become dangerous within hours. Know where you'd go to stay cool, keep extra water on hand, and check on older neighbors and anyone who relies on powered medical equipment. Never run a generator indoors or in the garage.

Flash flood

Never drive or walk through flowing water, even if it looks shallow. Stay out of washes and low crossings during and after storms, including storms miles away.

Water outage

Store at least one gallon per person per day for two weeks. Your water heater tank holds drinkable water too (turn off its gas or power before draining it).

Share your skills or needs

This is the part only our ward can do. Knowing who has a generator or medical training, and who may need extra help, lets us look after each other quickly.

Skills and resources

Medical or first-aid training, ham radio, chainsaw, generator, trailer, building trades, childcare, languages, and anything else you could offer.

Needs

Mobility limits, medical equipment that needs power, medications that need refrigeration, living alone, or no transportation.

Your privacy: Only the bishopric and the ward emergency preparedness leaders see what you share. Needs are never posted publicly. You can ask them to update or remove your information at any time.
